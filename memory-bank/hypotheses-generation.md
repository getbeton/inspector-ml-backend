# Founding Sales — Pete Kazanjy

A tactical guide for startup founders who must become their company's first salesperson. Written for B2B SaaS direct sales.

## Two epochs of startup sales

| Epoch | Stage | Who sells | Goal |
|-------|-------|-----------|------|
| 1. Evangelical sales | Pre-PMF | Founders | Learn, iterate, validate. Do things that don't scale. |
| 2. Scaling sales | Post-PMF | Hired reps | Repeat, scale, optimize. Professionalize the org. |

**Critical anti-pattern:** Hiring a VP of Sales or senior AE before you have product-market fit. Sales professionals are built to execute a known-good playbook. You don't have one yet. You need evangelical product management disguised as sales — and that's the founder's job.

## 10 mindset shifts for founders selling

1. **Plenty over scarcity** — The scarce resource is your time, not opportunities. Drop marginal deals fast; there are thousands more behind them. Spend good time with good opportunities.
2. **Activity above all** — More calls, demos, emails, proposals. Jump first, prepare midair. Template everything. Your default state should be productive action.
3. **Be direct** — Ask bluntly: do you have this problem, do you agree it needs solving, will you pay? Asking for the sale is not optional.
4. **Many shallow relationships** — You'll manage dozens to hundreds of concurrent conversations. CRM excellence is non-negotiable.
5. **Assume inevitability** — Frame conversations as *when*, not *if*. This builds consultative confidence and sets up future passes through the funnel.
6. **Unfazed by rejection** — A 20-30% win rate is solid for innovative solutions. Record the loss reason, share it, move on. Don't internalize.
7. **Record everything** — Set future-you up for success. Log key details after every call. Use CRM, notebooks, email capture, call recording.
8. **Expertise begets fearlessness** — Be a student of the vertical. Deep domain knowledge removes the urge to over-prepare and empowers you to just act.
9. **Glass-house transparency** — Instrument everything: emails, calls, presentations, win/loss notes. Accountability + shared learning = positive feedback loop.
10. **Sales is math** — Win rates, conversion ratios, average contract value, CAC. The sales leader who struggles with spreadsheets will struggle at scale.


## Problem-solution sales narrative

Build your narrative in this order. Each part builds on the previous one. If the prospect disagrees at any step, stop and address it — don't pitch past a dead audience.

1. **What is the problem?** — Articulate the business pain crisply. Test: pose it to someone in the industry — they should say "yes" and want to dig deeper.
2. **Who has it?** — Identify the person with functional responsibility and budget authority. Titles matter for targeting.
3. **What are the costs?** — Hard ROI (direct cost savings) and soft ROI (opportunity cost of non-solution). Understand unit economics of the problem.
4. **How is it currently solved?** — Four levels: no solution, solution via process, solution via service providers, solution via product. Know each path and its weaknesses — but also its strengths, for credibility.
5. **What has changed?** — A technology shift, market shift, or regulatory change that makes a new solution possible or necessary.
6. **How does the new solution work?** — Explain by comparison to what the prospect already understands. Keep it simple.
7. **Proof** — Quantitative (metrics that beat existing solutions on their own terms) + qualitative (testimonials, case studies, press). Third-party validation.


## Sales materials hierarchy

Build in this order — each artifact is a repackaging of the narrative for a different medium:

1. **Sales narrative** (internal document) — the master story
2. **Sales deck** (slides) — problem-solution structure, proof, pricing
3. **Cold outreach email templates** — concise, specific to the prospect's pain, include click targets (demo video, screenshots)
4. **Phone/voicemail scripts** — 30-90 second reformatting of narrative for spoken delivery
5. **Demo script** — Ordered by most important use cases first. Customize with prospect's data/context. Tell the story of a user's workflow, showing how your product improves each step.
